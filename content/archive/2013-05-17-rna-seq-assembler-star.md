<td><p> <strong>STAR: ultrafast universal RNA-seq aligner</strong></p>
<hr />
Dear All,<br />
<br />
I would like to formally introduce on SEQanswers our RNA-seq mapper <strong>STAR</strong>.<br />
Its advantages include:<br />
&#10;<ul>
<li>Very high mapping speed:<br />
on a modest 12-core cluster STAR maps 400 Million pairs per hour for human 2x100 Illumina reads (&gt;50 times faster than TopHat).</li>
<li>Accurate alignment of contiguous and spliced reads:<br />
in our tests on real and simulated data STAR showed better sensitivity and precision than TopHat.</li>
<li>Detection of polyA-tails, non-canonical splices and chimeric (fusion) junctions.</li>
<li>Mapping reads of any length:<br />
STAR can efficiently map reads of any length generated by current or emerging sequencing platforms, starting from ~15 bases (small RNA) and up to full length transcripts several kilobases long.</li>
<li>Thorough testing on large ENCODE datasets:<br />
STAR was used to map 64 Billion reads of long RNA-seq and 16 Billion reads of short RNA-seq, and will be used to map RNA-seq data in the next ENCODE phase.</li>
</ul>
STAR requires ~30GB of RAM for mapping to the human genome (could be reduced to 16GB in the "sparse" mode with some speed loss).<br />
<br />
More information can be found in out recent <a href="http://bioinformatics.oxfordjournals.org/content/29/1/15">paper</a>.<br />
If you decide to try it out, please download the <a href="http://code.google.com/p/rna-star/">latest STAR release</a>.<br />
I will be happy to answer any questions via SEQanswers, <a href="https://groups.google.com/d/forum/rna-star">STAR discussion forum</a>, or by e-mail:<a href="mailto:dobin@cshl.edu">dobin@cshl.edu</a><br />
<br />
Cheers<br />
