If you have read my previous post [Misconcept of epigenetics](/archive/misconcept-of-epigenetics/) , you will know that: histone modification is not epigenetics!

However, recently, four papers in *Science* actually showed that histone modifications actually have a component of inheritance:

[Causal role for inheritance of H3K27me3 in maintaining the OFF state of a *Drosophila* HOX gene](http://science.sciencemag.org/content/early/2017/03/15/science.aai8236)

[DNA sequence-dependent epigenetic inheritance of gene silencing and histone H3K9 methylation](http://science.sciencemag.org/content/early/2017/03/15/science.aaj2114)\
[Propagation of Polycomb-repressed chromatin requires sequence-specific recruitment to DNA](http://science.sciencemag.org/content/early/2017/03/15/science.aai8266)

[Transgenerational transmission of environmental information in *C. elegans*](http://science.sciencemag.org/content/356/6335/320)

and one paper in *Molecular Cell*:

## [SNF2 Family Protein Fft3 Suppresses Nucleosome Turnover to Promote Epigenetic Inheritance and Proper Replication](http://www.cell.com/molecular-cell/fulltext/S1097-2765(17)30098-9)

one more on *Nature Genetics:*

## [Stable Polycomb-dependent transgenerational inheritance of chromatin states in *Drosophila*](http://www.nature.com/ng/journal/vaop/ncurrent/full/ng.3848.html?WT.feed_name=subjects_genetics)

Interestingly, all the studies were carried out on model organisms: *Drosophila and C.elegans*!
